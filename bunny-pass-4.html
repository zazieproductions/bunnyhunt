8. The horali.ru link — same dead listing, third costume

You sent horali.ru/infobox_881005001333292108.html as a new exact match. I checked it. It isn't a shop, and it isn't new. Here is the whole reason, in one line:

Read the number in the URL

881005001333292108 = 88 + 1005001333292108.
That second half is item #3 in your very first message — the AliExpress listing already marked confirmed permanently discontinued in §2.1. horali.ru is serving you the same page you've already been sent, one mirror further out.

What the site actually is

  • It says so itself. horali.ru's own footer: "Данный сайт НЕ является интернет-магазином, а лишь содержит ссылки на официальный сайт продавцов" — "this site is NOT an online shop, it only contains links to the sellers' official sites." It is a Russian-language price-tracker / listing scraper.
  • No cart, no seller, no stock, no shipping. There is nothing on that page that can take money or send a bunny. The price it may show is scraped from AliExpress, where the item no longer exists.
  • It's the same family as the other link you sent. alirich.ru/pid991005001333292108.html — same item number, same template, same fake review block. Two domains, one dead listing. There will be more of these; lyvi.ru, imall.com and chinastock350.ru are already on the pile.
  • The pages are auto-generated filler. Open any random horali page and you'll find a title about children's winter coats above reviews of sandals, dated December 2021. That's a content farm indexing product IDs by the thousand, not a storefront.

New evidence today that the item is really dead

A small but solid thing I could test this pass: loading aliexpress.us/item/1005001333292108.html no longer lands on your bunny — AliExpress bounces it to 3256801146977356, a different product. When AliExpress stops resolving an ID to its own listing and starts substituting another item, the original is delisted at the database level, not just out of stock. That's why every mirror built on that ID is a photograph of a ghost.

A ten-second test for every link you find from now on

  1. Decode the number. 88 or pid99 or infobox_ in front of a 100500… AliExpress ID = mirror, no matter how shop-like the page looks.
  2. Find the cart. If you can't put it in a basket and see a checkout with your address in it, it is not a place you can buy from.
  3. Find a named seller, a shipping price and a return policy. A real listing has all three. A mirror has none.
  4. Read the footer. Almost all of these disclose what they are, in one sentence, at the bottom.
  5. Look at the reviews' dates. A wall of reviews from a single week in 2021 attached to a 2026 product page is generated filler.

Anything that passes those five is worth sending me. Anything that fails them costs you nothing to drop.
